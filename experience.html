Student Internship

Centre National d'Études Spatiales · Internship

Mar 2012 - Sep 2012 · 7 mos

Toulouse, Occitanie, France

Research and development of a LASER source stabilised at 1560,48 nm for the cooling of Rubidium atoms. This LASER was designed to be used as a possible source of the atomic interferometer of the STE-QUEST mission. It involved:

  • Creation of a complete optical LASER bench,
  • Characterisation of optic components and design of electronic devices for the stabilisation,
  • Elaboration of servo system.

My role was to develop and test an optical bench which main source was a diode at 780 nm with a narrow bandwidth, to obtain a stable source at 1560.48 nm.

The frequency doubling was made possible thanks to a non linear crystal called PPNL, a recent technology that needed to be characterised. The beam power was amplified using the injection principle and another LASER source, more powerful but less spectrally thin.

Two Rubidium cells were used to check the LASER frequencies by saturated absorption before and after the injection. Finally, a servo system (PID) has been designed to automatically adjust the voltage (and therefore the frequency) of the main source to prevent frequency shifting.
