From an early age, I have been passionate about space, technologies, and computer science.

Born in Toulouse (southern France) — a city renowned for aerospace and astrophysics — I pursued a Master’s degree in Astrophysics and Space Technologies at Paul Sabatier University.

During my studies, I gained exposure to a range of advanced technologies, including cryogenics, vacuum testing, optics, and electronics. At the same time, I began developing a strong foundation in computer science, with a particular focus on Python, C, and C++.

At the end of my degree, I completed a six-month internship at the French National Centre for Space Studies (CNES). My work there focused on designing an optical bench and developing a laser with stable enough frequency characteristics for use in atom cooling technologies required for interferometers and atomic clocks. This project formed part of a wider study aimed at identifying cost-effective solutions for the STE-QUEST mission.

Following this internship, I started working as a C/C++ Software Engineer at the Institute of Research in Astrophysics and Planetology (IRAP). In this role, I contributed to the development of several ground-segment software systems used to process data from two instruments on the TARANIS mission : IDEE and XGRE. My primary responsibility was to develop applications capable of transforming encrypted satellite data into calibrated files for scientific analysis.

Since 2016, I have been working at Ito World as a C++ Software Engineer, contributing to challenging projects focused on aggregating, managing, and processing real-time updates from transportation agencies. This work helps deliver reliable traffic information — including live schedule deviations and service disruptions — for public-facing applications such as journey planners and on-street displays.

I am particularly motivated by the opportunity to develop intelligent, safe, and sustainable services that improve mobility, accessibility, and overall quality of life.
